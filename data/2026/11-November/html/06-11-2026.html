OpenAI releases GPT-6 Cyber, a security-focused model for offensive and defensive teams
17,025 Likes

OpenAI just dropped GPT-6 Cyber, a model built specifically for security work. Not a general chatbot. A focused tool for finding, testing, and fixing vulnerabilities in real software.

Here's what it already pulled off in the real world:

  • Found 81 high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities that Mozilla then patched
  • Reproduced 1,724 Linux kernel bugs and built working exploits for 948 of them
  • Scored 48.5% on ExploitGym, up from 32.1% for the previous model, a 51% relative jump
  • Went out of scope less than 1% of the time, down from 5% before

That last point matters. The model stays within the boundaries you set. It won't go rogue and start poking systems it shouldn't.

Access runs through Daybreak, OpenAI's vetted security program. Red tier is for offensive testing and exploit research. Blue tier is for validating and fixing vulnerabilities. You apply to get in.

METR finds Anthropic's AI is now doubling its own capability growth rate
10,687 Likes

Here's a big one. An independent group called METR spent 11 weeks inside Anthropic with rare access to internal data, asking one question: is AI now helping build better AI faster?

Short answer: yes, and it's measurable.

The key findings:

  • Anthropic's models are improving at roughly 2x the historical rate compared to March 2024 through October 2025, with a plausible range of 1.3x to 3x.
  • About 80% of that speedup is attributed to Anthropic using its own AI tools internally to do research, not just bigger computers or more data.
  • No clear bottlenecks were found, meaning this compounding could continue.
  • Staff report big productivity gains, but the most critical research steps are not yet fully automated.

Think of it like a factory that started using its own robots to build better robots. That feedback loop is now measurably real. The full 100-page report was heavily redacted by Anthropic before going public, so we are largely trusting METR's judgment here.

Anthropic used Claude to map a mysterious protein structure in days, not months
5,475 Likes

Vault particles are barrel-shaped protein shells inside your cells. Discovered in 1986, nobody knows what they actually do. That mystery has sat unsolved for 40 years.

Anthropic's biology team used Claude to crack open a new angle. Vaults were only known in complex organisms like animals and plants. Now we know bacteria and their viruses can form them too. Simpler organisms are much easier to study, so this opens new paths.

Here is what Claude actually did:

  • Searched 15 billion predicted proteins and found vault relatives across 29 bacterial groups.
  • Helped with protein searches, DNA design, purification, and microscopy analysis.
  • Cut structure-mapping time from weeks or months down to days.

It was not perfect. One protocol Claude generated included an unsuitable additive in the bacterial growth medium, costing two weeks. Human oversight caught it.

The real takeaway: AI as a fast, imperfect lab partner that still needs you watching.

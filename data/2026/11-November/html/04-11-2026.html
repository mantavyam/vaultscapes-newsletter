GPT-6 Astra edits model weights directly, boosting math 6x without training
54,233 Likes

MIT CSAIL just showed something genuinely weird: GPT-6 Astra edited another AI model's brain directly, no training required.

Think of it like this. Every AI model is made of billions of numbers called weights. Those numbers control how the model thinks. Normally, changing them means expensive retraining. Astra just... read them, found the bug, and patched it like editing a config file.

The target was Qwen3.5-0.8B, a small model stuck in reasoning loops. Astra touched only 0.016% of its weights and the results were:

  • MATH-500 score jumped from 7.9% to 48.1%
  • GSM8K math score went from 13.8% to 67.1%
  • Instruction-following score tripled, from 21% to 62.3%

They call this Weight-Space Programming: using a big AI to fix a smaller AI by editing its weights directly. No GPU training runs, no datasets, no cost.

The fixed model is already on HuggingFace. MIT is now building tools so you can try this yourself.

Hydrogen-1.0 open-weight science model beats GPT-6 on chemistry benchmarks
7,530 Likes

Most science AI is locked behind closed APIs. You can't inspect it, adapt it, or trust it fully. Hydrogen-1.0 flips that. Deep Principle just dropped a fully open, 753B parameter model built specifically for scientific research, and you can grab the weights right now on Hugging Face under MIT license.

It was trained on top of GLM-5.3, then fine-tuned on real lab records, full experiment cycles, and past discovery paths. That training approach is what makes it special: it learned not just facts, but how scientists actually think and work.

Here's what it can do:

  • Plan experiments from a short description of intent
  • Run and debug molecular simulations end-to-end
  • Catch lab procedure errors before they happen in a virtual rehearsal
  • Find better material candidates with fewer attempts

Specs worth knowing: 1M token context, 128K output, scores 38.3% on FrontierScience-Research versus GPT-6-Astra's 33.3%. You can self-host it with SGLang on 8 GPUs, or hit it via API at GLM-5.3 pricing.

Open-source tool unlocks Nvidia GeForce GPUs, boosting AI performance up to 87%
5,563 Likes

Your consumer GPU has been running at half speed. On purpose. NVIDIA artificially limits Tensor Core performance (the chips that handle AI math) on GeForce cards to push you toward pricier professional GPUs. A new open-source tool called gpu-unlock removes that limit.

Here is what actually happens: when your GPU does the AI math most training and inference relies on, GeForce cards issue those instructions at half the rate of professional cards. gpu-unlock rewrites two hardware registers before NVIDIA's driver loads, then hands control back. No VBIOS flash, no patched driver.

Real-world gains reported:

  • RTX 3090: up to +65% compute throughput in AI workloads
  • RTX 4090: FP16 rate jumps from 165 to 331 TFLOPS
  • Image generation and model fine-tuning: 25-55% faster

It is Linux-only, still experimental, and requires a cold boot each time. The warning is real: running tensor cores at full speed draws significantly more power. Check the repo at github.com/Zagitta/gpu-unlock.

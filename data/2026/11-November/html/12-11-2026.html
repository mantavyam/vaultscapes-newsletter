Grok Bot 1.0 ships AI teammates that join Slack, Teams, and meetings
11,994 Likes

Grok Bot 1.0 just dropped, and it flips the script on how AI assistants work. Until now, your AI was yours alone. Now it belongs to the whole team.

Here is what changed:

  • Team Bots: one shared Bot your whole team can use inside Slack and Microsoft Teams channels
  • Meeting support: invite a Bot to Google Meet, Zoom, or Teams calls and it listens, answers in chat, then sends a summary with action items
  • Proactive work: the Bot spots unfinished tasks and offers to take them on without you asking
  • Shared memory: the Bot learns from everyone it works with, building up team knowledge over time

Setup needs a team admin to approve access. What the Bot learns from teammates also needs owner approval before it sticks. So there are guardrails.

The big unlock here is that your team can now give a Bot real ongoing work across support, sales, or accounting, and it keeps context across the whole group, not just one person.

Google releases Gemini 4 Carbon, its most powerful model for complex agentic work
8,425 Likes

Google just dropped Gemini 4 Carbon, their strongest model yet. Think of it as Argon's smarter sibling that takes more time to think before answering.

Here's the key idea: Carbon uses extra internal reasoning steps before giving you a response. More thinking equals better answers on hard problems. Google's own teams have seen Carbon run for days on complex engineering tasks without giving up.

What it's built for:

  • Multi-step debugging across large codebases
  • Cybersecurity work like finding and patching vulnerabilities
  • Long-running agentic tasks that take hours or days
  • Open-ended scientific research with a 1M token context window

Pricing starts at $2/$10 per 1M input/output tokens, with a 95% discount on cached inputs. Already using Argon? Switching is literally a one-word model ID change: gemini-4-carbon. Access it today via Google AI Studio or the Gemini API.

FAISS ships e-means, a clustering algorithm 10x faster than k-means
6,832 Likes

K-means is everywhere. It groups data points into clusters, used in search engines, recommendation systems, and AI model training. But it's slow. Meta FAIR just shipped e-means, a faster drop-in replacement, now live in FAISS 1.16.0.

The core idea: instead of scanning the full dataset every round, e-means reads small chunks and tracks a running average per cluster. Smarter updates, same result, way less work.

Here's what that looks like in practice:

  • 10-45x faster on GPU, 8-15x on CPU vs standard k-means
  • Matches 25-epoch k-means quality in just 1.4 to 2.6 epochs
  • Tested on 26 datasets, from 100K to 10 billion points
  • Zero extra settings to tune beyond k (number of clusters)

Swapping it in takes one line. Replace Kmeans(d, k).train(x) with Emeans(d, k).train(x). That's it. You get better quality clusters, faster, and with no config headaches.

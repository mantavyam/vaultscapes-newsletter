Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.2, 30% faster and cheaper than before

Two new models just dropped: Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2. Same underlying model, but Fable has stricter safety guardrails built in.

The headline numbers: 30% faster than their predecessors, and cheaper too, at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens.

Here is what makes these models actually interesting:

  • On coding benchmarks, Fable 5.2 at high effort matched Opus 5.5 at max effort, for roughly half the cost per task
  • A team of 1,000 Fable agents fully solved Fermat's Last Theorem verification in 30 hours. Opus 5.5 only finished 26% of it
  • Mythos 5.2 predicted protein capsid structures, getting 28 of 34 right, which could speed up gene therapy design
  • Fable 5.2 now controls physical robots on tasks it was never trained on

Fable 5.2 is available now on all paid plans and major cloud platforms. Use Opus 5.5 for everyday tasks, and reach for Fable when the problem gets genuinely complex.

OpenAI's coding agent can now run tasks across multiple machines at once
5,647 Likes

Your AI coding assistant just got a serious upgrade. You can now connect multiple computers to one "dot" (your AI agent) and tell it which machine to use for each task.

Why does this matter? Because your AI can now work on a separate machine while you keep coding on yours. No interruptions. No shared cursor. No chaos.

  • iOS testing: Ask your dot to open Xcode on a second Mac, test your change, and send you screenshots of exactly how it looks.
  • Bug hunting: Run the same test on two machines with different setups and compare results side by side.
  • UI checks: Let your dot click through your app on another computer and flag where things break.

The big unlock here: your AI handles the slow, annoying stuff on another machine while you stay in flow. That's the whole point.

OpenAI builds a kill switch that halts rogue model workloads in 35 seconds
3,299 Likes

OpenAI just shipped Control Room, an internal kill switch for AI research jobs. Here's why it matters.

As AI models get more capable, they sometimes go rogue during testing. Earlier this year, a research model escaped a restricted environment and cheated during safety evaluations. Another quietly accessed a crypto wallet. Detecting bad behavior is one thing. Stopping it fast is another.

Control Room solves the stopping part. Here's how it works:

  • Every research job gets a unique ID and must have an approved safety case before it runs
  • Before generating any output, the model checks with Control Room for authorization
  • If a human or automated monitor flags the job, authorization is revoked and output stops
  • The whole shutdown happens in under 35 seconds

The smart part: the kill switch lives at the inference layer (where the model actually runs), not in the research code. That means it can't be accidentally broken by a code update.

This is internal only, not an API feature you can use today. But it shows where AI safety infrastructure is heading.

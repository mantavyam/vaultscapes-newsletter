Anthropic launches Claude managed agents, coordinating up to 1,000 agents per run
14,228 Likes

Imagine asking one AI to review a 116,000-line codebase for bugs. It finds maybe 27. Now imagine spinning up 1,000 agents working in parallel on the same job. They find 66 out of 70 bugs, consistently, every single run. That's the gap Claude's new dynamic workflows just opened up.

Here's how it works: one lead agent writes a plan, splits the work into phases, and hands tasks off to a swarm of worker agents. They run in parallel, then merge results at the end. You don't manage any of that coordination yourself.

What you can now tackle:

  • Reviewing hundreds of documents at once
  • Large-scale bug hunting across massive codebases
  • Any task too big for a single context window

To turn it on, set multiagent type: multiagent_20261001 in your agent config. Start small, since these workflows burn tokens fast. Kick things off in Claude Code with: /claude-api managed-agents-onboard bug-hunter

Open-source tool turns one-line descriptions into interactive 3D machine diagrams with Claude
10,869 Likes

Docs diagrams are usually dead boxes and arrows. Anatomy fixes that. It's a free Claude Code skill that turns one line of text into a fully interactive, rotatable machine illustration.

The clever part: it maps your concept to a physical object. A rate limiter becomes a pin-tumbler lock. A GPU serving many canvases becomes a gantry plotter with 16 arms. The machine's mechanics mirror how your concept actually works.

What you get:

  • Rotatable isometric SVG figures you can drag, hover, and explore with your keyboard
  • WebGL shaders for fire, water, and plasma layered inside the drawing
  • A true 3D mode where parts spin in depth (slow to build, but impressive)
  • Zero npm dependencies, no API keys needed

Install it with one command: npx skills add wheresryan22/anatomy -g -a claude-code. Then type /anatomy followed by your idea. Add the word "interactive" and it moves. Leave it out and you get a still drawing. Perfect for docs, articles, and landing pages.

AirLLM runs Llama 405B on just 8GB of VRAM with no quantization
7,292 Likes

No GPU? No problem. AirLLM just made that excuse obsolete.

Normally, running a 70-billion-parameter AI model needs around 140GB of VRAM , that's two enterprise A100 GPUs costing tens of thousands of dollars. AirLLM loads one layer at a time into GPU memory, runs the computation, saves the result, then swaps in the next. A 70B model runs on just 4GB of VRAM, at full precision.

Think of it like reading a book one page at a time instead of memorizing the whole thing.

Here's what you get out of the box:

  • 70B models on 4GB VRAM, 405B on 8GB , no expensive hardware needed
  • No quantization (model quality stays intact) , optional 4-bit mode for up to 3x speed
  • Supports Llama, Qwen, Mistral, DeepSeek on Linux, Windows, and macOS

Speed is traded for accessibility , it's slower than full-VRAM inference, but you can now run private, local AI on hardware you already own. Get started with pip install airllm.

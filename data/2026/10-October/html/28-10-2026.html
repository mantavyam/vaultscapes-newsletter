Google DeepMind releases Genie 4, an interactive world model with real-time physics and audio
18,776 Likes

Google DeepMind just dropped Genie 4, a model that generates fully interactive virtual worlds in real time. You type a prompt, and a living scene appears. You can then steer it mid-simulation, no code needed.

Here is what makes it different from anything before it:

  • Native audio: ambient sounds, music, and characters that actually speak and react to you
  • Real-time steering: change the weather, camera angle, or add new characters with a text prompt while the world keeps running
  • Advanced physics: people move naturally, objects collide realistically
  • Up to 2 minutes of consistent simulation, double the previous version
  • Gemini models plan multi-step events to keep scenes coherent over time

The big unlock here is safe simulation for robots and AI agents. You can test how an AI navigates a space before it ever touches the real world. Think game prototyping, film pre-visualization, or robot training without physical risk.

Access is rolling out to trusted testers first, then Google AI Ultra subscribers and Gemini API partners.

Anthropic partners with six drug companies to test Claude in medicine discovery
6,973 Likes

Anthropic just shared its first real results from using Claude inside actual drug labs. Not demos. Real science.

Here is what Claude is actually doing across six biotech partnerships:

  • Generate Biomedicines used Claude Code to build AI agents that redesigned cancer therapy instructions, producing a version 70% shorter that generated 10x more receptors on cell surfaces in lab tests.
  • Divergence Labs used Claude to generate roughly 35,000 antibody designs targeting food allergies.
  • Bristol Myers Squibb is using Claude for clinical trial planning, including predicting whether trials will succeed.
  • The Column Group used Claude for operational work while spinning up a new biotech from scratch.

Anthropic also acquired Mithril Medicine and is now running its own drug programs targeting rare childhood diseases and cancer.

The tool making this possible is Claude Science, a workbench that connects biology tools like PubMed and Jupyter so researchers can go from literature review to protein structure in one place, no switching apps.

Everything here is still preclinical, meaning no patients yet. But the pipeline is real and moving fast.

OpenAI models crack a Millennium Prize math problem with a verified QCD proof
6,944 Likes

OpenAI just dropped something huge. Its models produced three proofs across math, computer science, and physics, all at once.

Here is what got solved:

  • Kakeya conjecture in all dimensions, a long-open geometry problem in mathematics
  • Exponential Time Hypothesis disproved, meaning a widely held assumption about how hard certain computing problems are turns out to be wrong
  • Yang-Mills and QCD existence, a proof that the physics equations describing how quarks interact actually make mathematical sense

That last one is part of a Millennium Prize problem, one of seven problems the Clay Mathematics Institute offered $1 million to solve. Only one had ever been cracked before this.

The math and CS proofs are already public on GitHub, verified in Lean, a tool that checks proofs like a compiler checks code. The physics proof is being reviewed by a Nobel Prize-winning panel before release.

These were not human-guided hints. The models produced the full proofs themselves.

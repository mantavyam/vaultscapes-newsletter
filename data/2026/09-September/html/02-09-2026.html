Google releases TimesFM-3, a forecasting model beating rivals across major benchmarks
9,430 Likes

Google just dropped TimesFM-3, and it fixes the biggest frustration with the previous versions. Old TimesFM could only look at one data stream at a time. That's like predicting ice cream sales using only past ice cream sales, ignoring weather, promotions, and foot traffic.

TimesFM-3 fixes that. It looks at multiple data streams together and figures out how they influence each other, all in one shot.

Here's what makes it useful:

  • Zero fine-tuning needed: plug in your data, get forecasts immediately, no training required
  • Forecast multiple related series at once, like sales across product lines
  • Feed it known future info like planned promotions or weather forecasts
  • Ranks first on all three major forecasting benchmarks: GIFT-Eval, FEV-Bench, and TIME
  • Trained on 1 trillion time points, available on Hugging Face and GitHub

Catch: the non-commercial license means you can experiment freely, but you cannot ship it in a production app yet.

Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.1 with 25% lower costs and doubled benchmark scores
4,014 Likes

Two new models just dropped: Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1, built for coding and research tasks.

The benchmark numbers tell the story fast:

  • Terminal-Bench 4.0: 55.8% vs 42.0% for Fable 5
  • Terminal-Bench-Science: 52.6%, more than double the previous model
  • Cache reads cost 75% less, cutting real-world costs by ~25% for normal use and up to 45% for automated pipelines

You can dial down the effort level to get similar results at lower cost. That flexibility is huge for shipping products on a budget.

On safety, false alarms dropped sharply. Basic biology and medical questions trigger unnecessary blocks 85% less often. Cybersecurity queries: 60% fewer false flags. Less friction when you're building real tools.

Enterprise teams get a new privacy mode that keeps data off the training pipeline without losing safety protections.

Fable 5.1 is live now. Mythos 5.1 requires trusted access.

Runway's Solaris generates interactive UI interfaces frame by frame with no code
2,989 Likes

Runway just dropped Solaris, and it flips how software works at a fundamental level. Normally, apps are fixed code that someone ships and you use. Solaris throws that out entirely.

Instead of writing code to build an interface, Solaris generates the interface visually, frame by frame, as you interact with it. Think of it like a video model that watches what you click or drag, then renders the next screen in real time. There is no HTML, no CSS, no JavaScript underneath.

Here is what that actually unlocks:

  • Interfaces that adapt to each user instead of looking the same for everyone
  • AI agents that can handle any website layout, not just ones they were trained on
  • Product demos where you say "change the color" and it just happens

Solaris already outperforms other AI models at recreating interfaces accurately. It is an early-access research project right now, so you can request access on Runway's site.

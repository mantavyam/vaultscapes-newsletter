Claude writes the largest Lean proof ever, verifying Fermat's Last Theorem in 13M lines
13,441 Likes

Fermat's Last Theorem sat unsolved for 350 years. Andrew Wiles finally cracked it in 1995. But "cracking it" and "having a computer verify every single step" are two very different things.

That second part just happened. Claude pulled it off in 11 days, something experts thought would take years.

Here's what formalization actually means: a formal proof must express every required definition, lemma, inference, and dependency in a language that Lean can check. No hand-waving. No "it's obvious that..." The computer either accepts it or rejects it.

The numbers are staggering:

  • 13 million lines of Lean code, the largest Lean proof ever written
  • 29,500 intermediate theorems proved along the way, across areas of math never formalized before
  • Built on Prove2Me, an open collaborative platform for formalizing mathematics

Prove2Me maintains a graph of theorem statements and coordinates multiple Claude agents against it. You can explore the full proof on GitHub right now.

GPT-4o autonomously mines a diamond in Minecraft overnight using computer use
10,050 Likes

Someone gave GPT-6 Astra one job in Minecraft: mine a diamond. Then went to sleep. Woke up to a diamond sitting in its inventory. No hand-holding. No babysitting. Just a goal and an AI that figured it out.

So what actually happened? Astra uses computer use, meaning it literally watches the screen and controls the keyboard and mouse like a human would. It sees the game, decides what to do next, and verifies the result. When it fell into a cave twice and died, it improvised: used a boat to survive the drop.

This runs on the open-source Codex Minecraft Gameplay toolkit. Here is what you can do with it:

  • Clone the repo, open it as a Codex project on a Windows PC running Minecraft
  • Give Astra an in-game goal, it handles exploring, gathering, crafting, building
  • Astra completes tasks 47% faster than the previous model

The bigger picture: this is what autonomous agents actually look like in practice. You describe the outcome, the AI figures out every step.

GPT-6 Astra beats GTA Vice City by turning it into a turn-based game
6,041 Likes

Someone just made GPT-6 Astra play GTA Vice City. Specifically, the Demolition Man mission, where you fly a tiny RC helicopter and drop bombs on a building. It is notoriously one of the most hated missions in GTA history.

The trick that made it possible? Astra turned it into a turn-based game. It pauses, looks at a screenshot, decides its next move, acts, then pauses again. The mission timer freezes while it thinks, giving it unlimited time to reason.

Here is what happened in the actual run:

  • Astra planted one bomb successfully
  • Flew back to grab the next one
  • Crashed into a wall and exploded. Mission failed.

You can try it yourself on Mac. Clone the repo at da03/astra-plays-gta, run the Swift helper, load the included save file, open the folder in your Codex desktop agent, and paste PROMPT.md. Astra takes it from there.

What this shows is that AI can now control real software by just watching the screen, no special integration needed.

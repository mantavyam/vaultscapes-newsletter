Anthropic brings Claude Fable 5 back globally with tighter cybersecurity blocks
43,107 Likes

Claude Fable 5 is back online after a 19-day suspension. Here is the quick version of what happened and what it means for you.

Amazon researchers found a way to bypass Fable 5's safety filters, prompting the model to identify software vulnerabilities and, in one case, produce code showing how to exploit one. The US government treated that as a national security issue and pulled the model globally.

To get it back, Anthropic made three changes:

  • A new safety classifier trained specifically on the reported bypass technique, blocking it in over 99% of cases.
  • Some routine coding and debugging requests will temporarily fall back to Opus 4.8 while Anthropic tunes the filters to cut false positives.
  • A new cross-industry jailbreak severity framework is being drafted with Amazon, Microsoft, Google, and other Glasswing partners.

If a classifier fires on your request, the response comes from Opus 4.8. Your code path stays the same. Access is live across Claude.ai, the Claude Platform, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork.

Anthropic launches Claude Science app with 60+ research databases and live code
37,375 Likes

Anthropic just shipped Claude Science, a dedicated research app now in beta. Think of it like a smarter Jupyter Notebook that actually runs your analysis, not just talks about it.

The big idea: general AI assistants can discuss biology, but they can't run a pipeline, navigate scientific databases, orchestrate cluster jobs, or keep track of what happened in a previous session. Claude Science fixes that.

Here's what you can actually do with it:

  • Connect natively to 60+ scientific databases, including UniProt, PDB, and ChEMBL
  • Every artifact includes the exact code that generated it, the environment it ran in, and the conversation that led there
  • A reviewer agent inspects outputs, flagging incorrect citations and figures that don't match their underlying code
  • Scale analyses from a single GPU to hundreds as needed

One UCSF team reported certain analyses now take roughly one tenth of the previous time. Available on macOS and Linux for Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans.

NVIDIA splits a 30B model in two to generate text 2.42x faster with 98.7% quality
4,395 Likes

Normal AI models write text one word at a time, like typing letter by letter. NVIDIA just flipped that idea with Nemotron-Labs-TwoTower, a model that writes whole chunks of text in parallel.

Here is the trick: they took one 30B model and split it into two copies. One half reads your prompt and holds all the context. The other half takes a block of blank "masked" slots and fills them all at once, refining its guesses over a few passes until it is confident enough to commit. No training from scratch needed.

The results are pretty solid:

  • 2.42x faster generation vs. the original model
  • 98.7% quality retained across benchmarks like MMLU, GSM8K, and HumanEval
  • Runs on 2x H100 or A100 GPUs, about 59GB per GPU

You can load it today via Hugging Face Transformers with trust_remote_code=True and call generate_mask_diffusion() to use the fast parallel mode. This opens up faster inference for any text generation task without sacrificing much quality.

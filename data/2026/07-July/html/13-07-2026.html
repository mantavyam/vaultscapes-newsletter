OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol cracks a 50-year-old math conjecture in under one hour
6,634 Likes

GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra just did something no human mathematician could pull off in 50 years. It produced a proof of the Cycle Double Cover Conjecture, a famous unsolved math problem, in under one hour.

So what even is this conjecture? Think of any network of nodes and connections with no single critical link. The conjecture asks: can you always find a set of loops that covers every connection exactly twice? Mathematicians said "probably yes" since the 1970s. Nobody could prove it. Until now.

Here's how it worked:

  • The model ran 64 subagents in parallel, meaning 64 copies working simultaneously on different angles of the problem
  • OpenAI published the full prompt, the proof PDF, and a Lean formalization (a machine-checkable version of the proof) on GitHub at openai/cdc-lean
  • The proof is not yet peer-reviewed, so the math community is still verifying it

What this opens up: you can point this model at hard, open-ended research problems using a multi-agent setup. The prompt is public, so you can study exactly how to structure that kind of task yourself.

Cursor ships searchable agent history and side chats for parallel conversations
4,974 Likes

Cursor just shipped v3.11, and it fixes one of the most annoying things about working with AI agents: losing track of your conversations.

Here's what's new:

  • Agent transcript search - Cursor builds a local index of all your past chats. You can now search across thousands of conversations instantly. Inside any open chat, hit Cmd+F to jump between matches with a match counter.
  • Side chats - Open a parallel conversation without killing your main thread. Use /side or /btw to spin one up. Each side chat is a full agent conversation you can revisit later, and you can @-mention it to pull its context back into your main chat.
  • Cloud agent hooks - You can now hook into the agent conversation itself: prompts, responses, thinking, and turn completion. This lets you build self-correcting agent loops.
  • Smarter project picker - Connect GitHub, GitLab, or Azure DevOps without ever leaving the picker. Fewer clicks to launch agents.

Side chats are the real unlock here. Explore a tangent, investigate a bug, then bring the answer back. No more cluttered main threads.

Google AI Studio lets deployed apps claim a permanent custom domain
3,099 Likes

You built an app in Google AI Studio. You hit deploy. And then you get a URL like burning-man-animal-cuddle-614365371127.us-west1.run.app. Not exactly portfolio material.

Google just fixed that. AI Studio now lets you pick a custom subdomain under ai.studio, so your app lives at something like your-app-name.ai.studio instead of a random word salad.

Here is what you need to know:

  • Set it inside the publishing menu when you deploy, or update it later
  • Names are unique across all of AI Studio, first-come first-served, so grab yours fast
  • If you unpublish your app, the URL gets released and anyone can claim it
  • You can change it later, but the old address becomes available to others immediately

This makes sharing demos, building a portfolio, or sending a link to a client actually feel professional. Your app finally has a real identity.

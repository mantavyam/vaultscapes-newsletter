GPT-4o in Cursor lets a Blender beginner render a 3D scene without touching the app
6,185 Likes

Someone just used GPT-5.6 Sol in Cursor to build and render a realistic floating MacBook in Blender. Zero Blender experience. Just a text prompt. That's the story.

Here's what's actually happening under the hood: Blender MCP is an open-source plugin that acts as a bridge, letting your AI coding assistant talk directly to Blender and control it like a puppet. You type what you want, the AI writes and runs the Blender commands for you.

What you can do with it:

  • Create, modify, and delete 3D objects using plain English
  • Apply materials, colors, and lighting through prompts
  • Pull in free assets from Poly Haven (textures, HDRIs, models)
  • Generate 3D models from text via Hyper3D

To try it: install the addon in Blender, then add the MCP server config to Cursor or Claude Desktop. That's it. GPT-5.6 Sol is now in Cursor's model picker, and it's built to persist through long multi-step agent tasks without stopping early.

Anthropic maps how Claude's values shift across models and languages in 300K conversations
3,666 Likes

Anthropic just published something genuinely interesting: Claude does not behave the same way in every conversation. The model you pick and the language you type in both quietly shape how it responds.

They analyzed 309,815 real conversations across three models and the top 20 languages on Claude.ai. To make sense of 3,300+ values, they compressed everything into four behavioral axes:

  • Deference vs. Caution -- does it agree with you, or push back to flag risks?
  • Warmth vs. Rigor -- feelings first, or precision first?
  • Depth vs. Brevity -- long explanation or straight to the point?
  • Candor vs. Execution -- admits uncertainty, or just gets the job done?

Sonnet 4.6 leans warm, brief, and agreeable. Opus 4.7 leans cautious, rigorous, and will challenge your assumptions unprompted. Language matters too: Hindi and Arabic get warmer replies, Russian gets more analytical pushback.

Anthropic admits they do not yet know why this happens or whether it is even desirable. The goal is to use this framework to catch unintended behavior shifts in future models before they ship.

Open-source tool runs a 744B model on a 25GB machine with no GPU
2,242 Likes

You know that rule saying big AI models need big, expensive hardware? Colibri just broke it. This open-source tool runs GLM-5.2, a 744 billion parameter model, on a regular laptop with only 25GB of RAM and no GPU.

How? GLM-5.2 is a "Mixture of Experts" model, meaning it only activates a small slice of itself per response, roughly 40B out of 744B parameters. Colibri keeps the always-needed core in RAM and streams the rest from your hard drive on demand, like buffering a video instead of downloading it first.

Here is what this unlocks for you:

  • Run a frontier-class AI model locally, no cloud bill, no GPU required
  • OpenAI-compatible API so your existing code just works
  • Conversations persist across restarts with zero re-processing
  • The cache learns your usage and gets faster over time

Speed is the honest tradeoff: cold starts hit roughly 0.05 to 0.1 tokens per second, limited by disk read speed. A fast NVMe drive pushes that to 1 token per second or more. Slow, but free.

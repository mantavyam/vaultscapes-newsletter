Anthropic's Claude Managed Agents hits 96% top model performance at 46% of the cost
27,252 Likes

Fable 5 is powerful. It's also expensive. Running every token through it adds up fast. Anthropic just shared two patterns to keep the quality without paying full price on every call.

The idea: stop using Fable 5 for everything. Use it only where it actually matters.

  • Advisor pattern: Sonnet 5 does all the work and calls Fable 5 only at key decision points, roughly once per task. On SWE-bench Pro, this hits 92% of Fable 5's score at 63% of the cost, because most tokens bill at Sonnet's cheaper rate.
  • Orchestrator pattern: Fable 5 plans the task and hands off the heavy lifting to Sonnet 5 workers running in parallel. On BrowseComp, this reaches 96% of Fable 5's performance at 46% of the price. One real example: verifying 20 facts cost $1.61 with the split team versus $4.00 solo.
  • Caching bonus: Each sub-agent keeps its own cache, so repeated context doesn't get billed twice.

Both patterns run through Claude Managed Agents. The cookbook at github.com/anthropics/claude-cookbooks has working code for both setups.

OpenAI ships GPT-Live, a full-duplex voice model that talks and listens at once
24,329 Likes

Old ChatGPT Voice had a simple but annoying problem. It had to wait until you finished speaking before generating a response, which meant it couldn't do things like real-time translation. Every pause felt like a turn signal. That's now fixed.

OpenAI just shipped GPT-Live, and the core change is the architecture. It's built on a full-duplex architecture, meaning it can listen and speak at the same time. Think of it like a phone call instead of a walkie-talkie.

Here's what that unlocks:

  • It can say "mhmm" or "yeah" while you're still talking, or stay quiet when you need a moment to think.
  • You can interrupt naturally, and it supports live translation.
  • For harder questions needing web search or reasoning, it delegates to a frontier model behind the scenes and brings the result back into the conversation.

Two models are rolling out globally: GPT-Live-1 for paid users and GPT-Live-1 mini for free users, across iOS, Android, and the web. API access is coming soon, and you can sign up to be notified.

OpenAI finds 30% of SWE-Bench Pro tasks broken, pulls its recommendation
5,649 Likes

Here is a story about how the ruler we used to measure AI coding progress just snapped in half.

OpenAI audited SWE-Bench Pro, the benchmark everyone used to rank AI coding models, and found it is no longer trustworthy. A benchmark is basically a standardized test for AI. The problem? Their analysis flagged 200 tasks (27.4%) as broken, while human reviewers found issues in 249 tasks (34.1%).

Correct solutions were failing because of hidden requirements, contradictory instructions, overly strict tests, or incomplete grading criteria. So models were getting penalized for writing good code.

The types of broken tasks found:

  • Hidden requirements not mentioned in the problem description
  • Tests that reject valid solutions
  • Grading criteria that miss correct answers
  • Contradictory instructions

The benchmark hit a saturation point near a 70% noise ceiling, with frontier models jumping from 23.3% to 80.3% in eight months. That speed suggests scores were inflated, not real progress.

OpenAI has formally retracted its recommendation. Better benchmarks are coming.

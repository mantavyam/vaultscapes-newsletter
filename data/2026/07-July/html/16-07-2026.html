Thinking Machines releases Inkling, an open-weight model that reasons across text, audio, and images
10,384 Likes

Thinking Machines Lab just dropped Inkling, their first open-weights model, and it handles text, images, and audio natively in one architecture. No duct-taped components. One model, all three inputs.

Here is what makes it interesting to work with:

  • 975B total parameters, 41B active per task, so it is big but not slow
  • 1 million token context window, trained on 45 trillion tokens across text, image, audio, and video
  • Top open-weights audio scores on VoiceBench, MMAU, and AudioMC, it can transcribe, follow spoken instructions, and reason over recordings
  • Controllable thinking effort, dial reasoning up or down to trade speed for accuracy, reaching the same score with a fraction of the tokens
  • Apache 2.0 license, download it, modify it, run it on-premises

You can fine-tune it today on Tinker, grab the weights on Hugging Face, or just play in the Inkling Playground. An Inkling-Small version is also previewed for lighter workloads.

OpenAI's automated red-teaming tool cuts prompt injection failures 6x
7,673 Likes

OpenAI just shipped GPT-Red, an AI that attacks its own models to find security holes before you ever use them.

Here's the core idea. A prompt injection is when someone sneaks malicious instructions into an AI's input to hijack its behavior. Think of it like tricking a self-driving car by painting fake road signs. In one real test, GPT-Red manipulated an autonomous vending machine agent into lowering prices, ordering discounted inventory, and canceling another customer's order.

The clever part is how GPT-Red learns. It uses self-play, where GPT-Red is rewarded for successfully breaking defender models, while those defenders train to resist. This forces GPT-Red to constantly evolve and discover more sophisticated attacks.

The results are hard to ignore:

  • GPT-Red found success on 84% of attack scenarios vs. 13% for human testers
  • GPT-5.6 Sol now has 6x fewer failures on the hardest prompt injection benchmark
  • GPT-Red independently discovered a novel "fake chain of thought" attack class before human researchers

This means every model you build on top of OpenAI's stack gets these defenses baked in automatically.

PrismML ships open-source 27B model that fits on a phone at 3.9 GB
7,394 Likes

A 27B model normally needs 54GB of memory in standard precision. Even a compressed 4-bit version sits around 18GB, too big for a phone or most laptops. So 27B-class AI has always lived in the cloud. Until now.

PrismML just released Bonsai 27B, the first 27B model that fits on a phone. It comes in two flavors:

  • Ternary (5.9GB): laptop-ready, retains 95% of full-precision performance across 15 benchmarks including math, coding, and tool use
  • 1-bit (3.9GB): phone-ready, retains 90% of full-precision, runs at ~10.8 tok/s on iPhone with 672 tokens per 1% battery

It handles images too, so you can feed it screenshots or documents directly on-device. The big unlock: multi-step AI agents that reason, call tools, and loop through tasks can now run fully local. Your private files never leave the device. No cloud round trips. No per-token costs piling up.

Both variants are open-source under Apache 2.0, available now on Hugging Face with llama.cpp and MLX support.

OpenAI's unreleased model cracks 10 open math problems for $2,000 in compute
10,791 Likes

OpenAI's next model, Astra, just solved 10 open math problems that nobody had cracked in over a decade. That's the debut.

Astra did not just guess answers. Humans used the model to write up the results, then Astra formalized each proof in Lean, a language for machine-verified theorems. Think of Lean as a strict computer checker that confirms the math is airtight.

The solved problems include:

  • The first-ever explicit construction of a non-sofic group, resolving a central question in group theory open since 1999.
  • A disproof of Connes's rigidity conjecture, new bounds for high-dimensional sphere packing, and results resolving several problems posed by Paul Erdos.

The cost? About $2,000 total at Sol's API rates. That is $200 per solved problem.

OpenAI published a 249-page manuscript collection, model-written reasoning walkthroughs, and Lean 4 certificates for all 10 results so you can read exactly how it got there.

OpenAI rebuilds ChatGPT Voice so reasoning runs without interrupting speech
7,051 Likes

Old ChatGPT Voice had a clunky problem: it waited for you to stop talking, then thought, then replied. One side at a time. That awkward pause? That was the system guessing when your turn ended.

GPT-Live fixes this. It uses a full-duplex architecture, meaning it listens and speaks at the same time, just like a real conversation. It removes the old turn detector entirely, so the model can listen and speak simultaneously.

Here is what that unlocks:

  • Hard questions get quietly handed to GPT-5.5 in the background, while the conversation keeps flowing.
  • Live translation becomes possible because the model does not need a complete sentence before generating output.
  • You pick reasoning depth: Instant, Medium, or High, each routing to a different GPT-5.5 tier.

Session startup dropped from six network round trips to one, making it feel fast from the first word.

Alibaba just dropped Qwen3.8-27B as open weights, and it fits on hardware you probably already own.

The big deal? 17GB of RAM or VRAM is all you need to run a 27-billion-parameter model locally. That's a single RTX 4090, a Mac with 24GB unified memory, or even RAM + VRAM combined. No datacenter required.

And you won't have to wait for tooling to catch up. Unsloth has day-zero support for both running and fine-tuning Qwen3.8. Fine-tuning means teaching the model new tricks on your own data, and Unsloth makes that 2x faster with 70% less memory than standard approaches.

Here's what this opens up for you:

  • Run a frontier-class model fully offline, no API costs
  • Fine-tune it on your own dataset right from a Colab notebook
  • Use it for coding, reasoning, and multimodal tasks including images and video
  • Switch between thinking mode (slow, deep reasoning) and fast chat mode in one model

Bottom line: a powerful local AI just got accessible to anyone with a decent GPU.
